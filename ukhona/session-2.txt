Session 2 of an ongoing oral history project continued documenting "Daddy's" (Patrick Muzale's) life story, covering his relationship with Uncle George, early multi-school education journey, the departure of his mother Ikesa, and his entry into the East African Railways. Education Timeline
- Baptism: December 26 (Church of Uganda); triggered transition to formal schooling. [Citation]
- Greek Orthodox School (~1949): Taught English from P1; left after one year due to increased fees and uniform cost changes. [Citation][Citation]
- Catholic School (Isegiro/Namunyunya): Hid Protestant identity to gain admission; scored 100/100 in Math at P2 external exams; expelled when baptism status was discovered. [Citation][Citation]
- Namalemba (Anglican, P3–P4, ~1951–52): Of 44 students, only 3 passed the P4 external exam to enter P5; Muzale passed on his first attempt, the only first-timer to do so. [Citation][Citation]
- Namutumba (P5–P6, ~1952–54): Entry required a bribe to the headmaster (money passed through Muzale, headmaster feigned ignorance calling it payment for a ruler). [Citation][Citation]
- Muiri Junior Secondary (~1955–57): Arrived as an academic standout but ranked 27th of 27 initially; rescued when carpentry was dropped from assessments. Left without a Junior Leaving Certificate. [Citation][Citation]
---
Uncle George
- Approximately 2 years younger than Muzale; name "Ntaro" suggests birth around 1945 (linked to King George VI, the reigning monarch). [Citation][Citation]
- Attended Bukonta Community Training School, which taught English from the start — giving him superior English and pronunciation. [Citation][Citation]
- Was exempt from strict discipline applied to Muzale by their father Abimelech; derived amusement from Muzale's punishments. [Citation][Citation]
- Never forgave their mother Ikesa for leaving; George was described as more emotionally damaged by her departure than Muzale. [Citation]
- Later entered the railways through Muzale's assistance. [Citation]
---
Ikesa's Departure & Family Dynamics
- Ikesa left the family during a famine period around 1952, returning to Bukono. [Citation][Citation]
- Abimelech's frequent absences as a driver (working near Chitantaro/Lake Choga area) left Ikesa alone, creating social instability. [Citation][Citation]
- Ikesa's mother Kaguna operated a shrine (spiritual practice), receiving clients who brought millet offerings in exchange for counsel — a source of community prestige. [Citation][Citation]
- Age estimates: Abimelech born 1911 (accurate via baptism records); Ikesa estimated born 1922–1924 (no Christian records, zero accuracy confirmed). [Citation][Citation]
- A prior infant death before Muzale was noted; Muzale was not the firstborn. [Citation]

Railway Career
- 1958: Recruited to the East African Railways Traffic Training School, Jinja, competing against candidates from across East Africa; placed among 16–20 trainees. [Citation][Citation]
- Entry facilitated by Amisi (a Muslim relative of Ikesa), who was head man at the school and had connections to tutors. [Citation][Citation]
- 1960: Graduated and posted to Nairobi (the railways' headquarters and advanced training center). [Citation][Citation]
- 1965: Final year in Nairobi, after independence. [Citation]
- Met lifelong friend Karinudge as a dormitory roommate in Jinja; both posted to Nairobi together. [Citation][Citation]

Clan & Regional Context
- Bugabula was the dominant clan/county with 52 sub-counties; other counties had only 4–6. [Citation][Citation]
- Clans discussed include Baisengobi, Bagabula, and Igaga (sons of Baisin mothers); ruling clans are distributed across different geographical areas, not one-to-one with geography. [Citation][Citation]
- Luganda served as the lingua franca for missionaries across both Protestant and Catholic denominations. [Citation][Citation]
